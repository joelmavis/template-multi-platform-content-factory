# SAMPLE / 示例: A clearer next step for every content draft

This fictional example explores a simple editorial review habit. It makes no claims about measured results and does not describe a real team. The goal is to give editors a concrete way to discuss what should happen after a review meeting.

## Start with the reader

A topic can sound clear while still serving several different audiences. Before editing a sentence, write down one reader and the problem they are trying to solve. “An editor preparing next week’s topics” gives a discussion more direction than “anyone interested in content.” The description can change as the team learns more, but it should be specific enough to guide the next revision.

## Name the practical takeaway

Next, describe what the reader could try after reading. For a review checklist, the action might be writing a single sentence that explains the draft’s main point. For a longer explanation, it might be comparing two possible approaches. This note is a planning aid, not a promise that every reader will take the action or achieve a particular result.

## Assign the next edit

A comment such as “make this clearer” leaves room for different interpretations. A more concrete note identifies a section, an intended change, and the role responsible for it. Add a due date that the owner has agreed to. Keep unresolved questions visible instead of quietly turning assumptions into facts.

## Adapt the shared idea

One topic does not require identical copy across channels. A short thread can present a sequence. A professional post can discuss the team decision. A community post can invite people to explain their own experience, without inserting a sales pitch. A Chinese article should be written for its readers rather than translated word by word from an English version.

## Review before publication

A human editor still needs to check factual claims, permissions, tone, and the final presentation. Automated checks can flag length and missing metadata, but they cannot approve a message on behalf of a person. Try recording the reader, takeaway, and next owner during a review, then discuss whether those notes helped the team make its next decision.
